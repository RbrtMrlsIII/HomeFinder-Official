# 5.5G.6A — House Topology Clarification

## Purpose

This clarification converts the established project intent into an explicit machine-readable topology rule so a future session cannot mistake the three-house allocation for a fully connected three-node graph.

## Confirmed topology model

**House 1 is the sole inter-house transit hub.**

```text
              House 2
             /       \
            /         \
        House 1       NO DIRECT LINK
            \         /
             \       /
              House 3
```

The operational interpretation is:

- House 1 = Public / Hero
- House 2 = Broker + Operations
- House 3 = Seekers + Owners
- House 2 does **not** directly connect to House 3.
- House 3 does **not** directly connect to House 2.
- A House 2 ↔ House 3 journey, when logically authorized, must pass through House 1.

## Important distinction

This is **not** an assertion that House 1↔House 2 or House 1↔House 3 is already physically proven.

Those two legs remain candidate-only until canonical/staged geometry provides:

1. a unique source boundary;
2. a unique destination boundary;
3. an authored physical portal;
4. continuous walkable geometry;
5. successful route validation.

The topology contract therefore distinguishes:

`logical reachability` ≠ `physical direct connection` ≠ `validated runtime route`.

## Code-level reconciliation

The application already separates destination/role surfaces:

- `broker-hq.html` is a broker-only product route.
- `admin.html`, `moderator.html`, and `staff.html` are operations routes.
- `market.html` and `profile.html` are product routes for seeker/owner/broker roles as defined by the route authority.

The `feature-wiring-matrix.json` places Broker HQ and Operations in House 2 and Market/Profile in House 3. That allocation is a presentation/semantic contract, not a physical doorway graph.

The existing `5.5d-physical-travel-contract.json` previously listed all six directed house pairs as `TO_AUTHOR`. That vocabulary is too permissive for the clarified world model. It must **not** be interpreted as permission to author House 2↔House 3 direct routes.

## Runtime rule to carry forward

Future route work must use this rule:

> If the destination belongs to House 2 and the current physical house is House 3, route through House 1. If the destination belongs to House 3 and the current physical house is House 2, route through House 1. Never invent a direct House 2↔House 3 portal.

Role/access decisions remain application-owned. Physical traversal decisions remain SH3D/route-validation-owned.

## Current physical status

| Leg | Physical status | Runtime |
|---|---|---|
| House 1 → House 2 | Candidate only | Blocked |
| House 2 → House 1 | Candidate only | Blocked |
| House 1 → House 3 | Candidate only | Blocked |
| House 3 → House 1 | Candidate only | Blocked |
| House 2 → House 3 | No direct connection accepted | Blocked |
| House 3 → House 2 | No direct connection accepted | Blocked |

## Investigation conclusion

The deeper code/contracts support the **semantic separation** of House 2 and House 3, but the application route contracts should not be allowed to imply direct physical connectivity.

The next topology investigation should therefore focus narrowly on:

**House 1 ↔ House 2** and **House 1 ↔ House 3** independently.

No work should be spent trying to discover or manufacture a House 2 ↔ House 3 corridor.
